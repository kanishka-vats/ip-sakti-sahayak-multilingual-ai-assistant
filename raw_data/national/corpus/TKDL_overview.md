# Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) — Prior-Art Reference

## Section 1: What TKDL is
The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is a pioneering Indian initiative
jointly maintained by the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) and
the Department of AYUSH (now Ministry of Ayush). It documents traditional medicinal
knowledge from Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Yoga in a structured, multilingual format
covering formulations, ingredients, therapeutic uses and classical references.

## Section 2: Purpose — defensive protection against biopiracy
TKDL functions as defensive prior art. By recording traditional formulations in a
format searchable by international patent examiners, it prevents the grant of patents
over knowledge already in the public domain. Landmark outcomes include the revocation
or withdrawal of patents relating to turmeric (wound healing), neem (fungicidal uses)
and basmati-related claims before the USPTO and EPO.

## Section 3: Access and usage
TKDL access is granted to international patent offices under non-disclosure and
non-use agreements for examination purposes only. It is not an open public database.
Indian applicants and researchers route TKDL prior-art searches through CSIR-TKDL
institutional channels. This assistant provides TKDL prior-art pointers only; it does
not grant TKDL access.

## Section 4: Patent-law interface — Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970
Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970 excludes from patentability "an invention which
in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known
properties of traditionally known component or components". A formulation identical to
a TKDL-recorded composition faces a Section 3(p) objection; patentability requires a
demonstrable technical advance such as enhanced efficacy under Section 3(d) standards,
novel synergy, or a new delivery mechanism supported by data.

## Section 5: Interaction with the Biological Diversity Act, 2002
Where a TKDL-linked formulation uses Indian biological resources, the Access and
Benefit Sharing (ABS) obligations of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 apply in
addition: prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) under
Section 3 for foreign access, Section 4 for transfer of research results, and
Section 6 before applying for intellectual property rights. TKDL clearance and NBA
approval are parallel, independent requirements.
